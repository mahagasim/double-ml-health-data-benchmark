# Literature matrix

| Citation | Year | Problem addressed | Estimator / framework | Estimand | Key assumptions | Nuisance learner | Theoretical / methodological contribution | Empirical or simulation component | Limitation / boundary | Relevance to this project |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rosenbaum & Rubin, *Biometrika*, “The Central Role of the Propensity Score in Observational Studies for Causal Effects” | 1983 | Covariate imbalance in observational treatment assignment | Propensity-score adjustment framework | Causal treatment effects under assignment mechanisms | Strong ignorability / unconfoundedness plus positivity | Parametric/nonparametric propensity estimation conceptually possible | Shows treatment assignment can be balanced through the propensity score under the stated assumptions | Observational-study examples | Propensity scores do not solve unmeasured confounding | Identification and propensity-score foundation |
| Bang & Robins, *Biometrics*, “Doubly Robust Estimation in Missing Data and Causal Inference Models” | 2005 | Model misspecification in outcome or treatment mechanisms | Doubly robust / augmented estimation | Causal effects and missing-data parameters | Identification assumptions plus correct specification of at least one of the paired nuisance models for DR consistency | Primarily parametric working models in the paper | Formalizes “two chances” logic of double robustness in causal models | Simulations and cardiovascular trial application | DR is not protection when both nuisance models fail badly; extreme propensity scores can still be problematic | AIPW benchmark and conceptual precursor to DML-IRM score |
| Crump, Hotz, Imbens & Mitnik, *Biometrika*, “Dealing with Limited Overlap in Estimation of Average Treatment Effects” | 2009 | Imprecision and specification sensitivity under limited overlap | Propensity-score-based optimal subsample / trimming logic | ATE in an overlap-defined population | Unconfoundedness and meaningful overlap in target population | Propensity score | Formal treatment of overlap and optimal subpopulations for precise ATE estimation | Methodological examples | Trimming changes the target population/estimand | Justifies dedicated weak-overlap scenario and explicit treatment of trimming as sensitivity |
| Belloni, Chernozhukov & Hansen, *Review of Economic Studies*, “Inference on Treatment Effects after Selection among High-Dimensional Controls” | 2014 | Post-selection bias and inference with many controls | Double-selection / post-LASSO methods | Treatment effect in approximately sparse high-dimensional models | Approximate sparsity and regularity conditions | LASSO/post-LASSO | Shows how naive selection can invalidate treatment-effect inference and develops valid high-dimensional procedures | Empirical application plus theory | Sparsity/linear-structure dependent; not a general nonlinear ML solution | Motivates Scenario B and the LASSO nuisance learner |
| Chen & Guestrin, *KDD*, “XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System” | 2016 | Scalable nonlinear prediction with additive boosted trees | Gradient-boosted decision trees | Predictive target, used here only for nuisance functions | Supervised-learning assumptions; causal identification comes from the DML model, not XGBoost | XGBoost | Develops a regularized scalable tree-boosting system with sparsity-aware computation | Large-scale predictive benchmarks | Prediction quality does not itself imply valid causal inference | Optional boosted-tree nuisance sensitivity; retained but not used in the frozen primary benchmark |
| Chernozhukov et al., *The Econometrics Journal*, “Double/Debiased Machine Learning for Treatment and Structural Parameters” | 2018 | Regularization and overfitting bias from ML nuisance estimation | Neyman-orthogonal scores + cross-fitting (DML1/DML2) | Low-dimensional causal/structural parameters including ATE/ATTE | Identification assumptions plus nuisance-rate/product-rate and regularity conditions | Broad ML class: LASSO, RF, boosting, neural nets, etc. | Establishes the generic DML framework and root-N/asymptotically normal inference under weak conditions | Three empirical examples plus theoretical applications | DML does not fix identification, positivity, or arbitrary poor nuisance learning | Core theoretical foundation; PLR partialling-out is the primary DML score and IRM/ATE is retained as a secondary extension |
| Zivich & Breskin, *Epidemiology*, “Machine Learning for Causal Inference: On the Use of Cross-Fit Estimators” | 2021 | Bias/inference problems when epidemiologic causal estimators use flexible ML | Cross-fitted g-computation/IPW/DR estimators including AIPW/TMLE comparisons | Average causal effect | Standard causal identification assumptions; adequate finite-sample behavior | Ensemble ML / Super Learner and parametric models | Health/epidemiology simulation evidence on why cross-fitting matters with ML nuisances | 2,000 simulated samples, nonlinear DGPs | Larger samples may be needed; findings depend on studied DGPs/learners | Direct health-methods precedent and Monte Carlo design reference |
| Chernozhukov, Escanciano, Ichimura, Newey & Robins, *Econometrica*, “Locally Robust Semiparametric Estimation” | 2022 | Sensitivity of semiparametric moments to high-dimensional/nonparametric first steps | Locally robust / orthogonal moments | Broad semiparametric causal/economic parameters | Moment identification plus regularity conditions | Flexible high-dimensional first-step methods | General construction of orthogonal moments reducing first-step/model-selection bias | Theory with applications | General theory is broader and more technical than needed for the core benchmark | Deepens explanation of Neyman orthogonality beyond package mechanics |
| Chernozhukov, Newey & Singh, *Econometrica*, “Automatic Debiased Machine Learning of Causal and Structural Effects” | 2022 | Debiasing functionals of high-dimensional regressions | Automatic debiased ML / Riesz representer approach | Broad causal/structural effects including ATE/ATT | Functional and learner-rate regularity conditions | LASSO, RF, NN, boosting and others | Extends debiasing beyond hand-derived corrections and provides robust inference | NSW job training and Nielsen demand applications | More general than required for this portfolio project | Positions DML as part of a broader modern debiased-ML literature |

## Pedagogical bridge

Yuksel & Aydede, *Causal Inference and Machine Learning: In Economics, Social, and Health Sciences*, Chapter 19, is used as a pedagogical bridge for Double LASSO, partialling-out, DML1/DML2, orthogonalization, and cross-fitting. Primary claims in the project should still be anchored to the peer-reviewed methodological literature above.
